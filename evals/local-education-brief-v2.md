BATCH SNAPSHOT

- Batches present: S09-or-W12 (CleverDeck, conflicting), S11, S12, S13, W14 (×2), S15, W16 — then a long gap in this dataset — W24, S24, F24, W25 (×5), Spring 2025 (×3), S25 (×2), and one labeled Summer 2026.
- The cohort mix is barbell-shaped: eight companies from 2009–2016 and thirteen from 2024–2026, with essentially nothing from 2017–2023 in this evidence set. Any "era comparison" therefore compares the mobile/web edtech wave directly against the post-LLM wave.
- Theme 1: the early cohort split between consumer self-serve learning (Codecademy, CodeCombat, Cambly, Outschool, CleverDeck) and institutional plumbing (Clever, Panorama, GiveCampus) — and the institutional side produced the one confirmed exit in the evidence (Clever, "$500m" per Rafael Garcia's bio).
- Theme 2: the 2024–2026 cohort is overwhelmingly AI-native and concentrated on the delivery layer — tutoring (Tegore, Chiron, Excellence Learning, Mathos), content transformation (Miyagi Labs, Educato, General Learning), and teacher/admin workflow (Frizzle, Edexia, Scout, Risely).
- Theme 3: founder profiles have bifurcated into very young repeat builders (Edexia at 17–18, Michael Tong at 19, Daniel Martinez covered by Forbes at 18) and senior operators pivoting into education (McKinsey/Bain, Salesforce Education Cloud, Airwallex, Coinbase/Tesla/Meta).

REPEATING PATTERNS

- **Institutional infrastructure wedge recurs across a decade.** Selling to districts/universities rather than learners shows up in Clever (S12), Panorama (S13), GiveCampus (S15), then Scout (W25, "digital infrastructure for America's schools") and Risely AI (S25, via Salesforce Education Cloud/UC Regents credentials).
- **Math and quantitative pedagogy as founder-market fit.** A striking share of the recent set is math-specific and founded by trained mathematicians/physicists: Chiron (two Oxford mathematicians, neurosymbolics for math), Excellence Learning (Oxford Maths + Polytechnique physics + physics-tournament winner), Mathos (career ML researcher), plus Shyam Sai's Midwest Math Circle at Frizzle.
- **Second-time learning-product founders.** Repeat builders reuse the same domain: Nick Winter (Skritter → CodeCombat), Edexia's Gibbon and Wang (prior EdTech to 200k ARR → Edexia), Michael Tong (RevisionDojo → General Learning), Rafael Garcia (Clever → Kernel, exiting the category).
- **Elite-consultancy operator into edtech GTM.** Kim Rants (Alice.tech, McKinsey Associate Partner), Alex Southmayd (Bloomy, McKinsey EM + Lyft growth), Shahryar Abbasi (Risely, Bain + Airwallex GM) — consistent pattern of senior enterprise/growth operators pairing with a domain or technical co-founder.
- **CEO-GTM / CTO-model split.** Most 2024–2025 pairs are explicitly two-role: Frizzle (PM CEO / ML CTO), Educato (CEO with no stated technical background / ML-M.Sc. CTO), Risely (GTM CEO / product-domain CPO), Scout, Chiron, Miyagi Labs, Edexia.
- **Geographic spread beyond the Bay.** The category is no longer SF-only: Edexia (Brisbane), Alice.tech (Copenhagen), Chiron (London), General Learning (Hong Kong), Scout/Frizzle/Miyagi Labs (New York), GiveCampus (Washington DC).

EMERGING / NEW PATTERNS

- **Chatbots replaced by structural approaches.** The newest math companies lean on specific technical bets rather than generic LLM wrappers — Chiron on "neurosymbolics for math," Miyagi Labs on video→interactive-course transformation, Shyam Sai with an LLM-applications patent. Older cohort wedges were interaction-design bets instead (CodeCombat's multiplayer game, Skritter's spaced repetition).
- **The buyer is shifting from IT/data plumbing to teacher and grading workflow.** Clever and Panorama sold connectivity and measurement; Frizzle, Edexia, and Scout target educator-facing work directly, with Risely aiming at university systems-of-record.
- **New founder archetype: the credentialed insider.** Sadia Saifuddin (built Salesforce's Education Cloud SIS; former voting UC Regent) and Alex Southmayd (TFA teacher + Manhattan Prep instructor + Lyft growth) represent domain-insider founders rather than the outsider-consumer-app profile of the early 2010s.
- **New founder archetype: the teenage repeat founder.** Edexia (ARR at 17–18), General Learning's 19-year-old CTO, and Units' Daniel Martinez ("kicked out of high school… covered on Forbes at 18") have no analogue in the older companies in this set.
- **New wedge: the school itself.** Units (Spring 2025) — Daniel Martinez, Founder/CEO (https://www.linkedin.com/in/danielmartinezdev), building "the school that doesn't hold back ambitious kids like me" — is an institution-building bet rather than software sold to incumbents; nothing in the older cohort matches this.
- Caveat: with a 2017–2023 gap in this set, the era comparison rests on eight older companies whose bios are largely empty; a fuller 2018–2023 sample would be needed to confirm these shifts.
