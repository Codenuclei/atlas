BATCH SNAPSHOT

The set spans roughly fifteen years of YC batches, but it is heavily barbelled. The early tail is thin and consists of durable, well-known names: Codecademy (S11), Clever (S12), Panorama (S13), CodeCombat and Cambly (W14), GiveCampus (S15), and Outschool (W16). Between 2016 and 2024 the evidence shows a near-total gap, which likely reflects the industry-wide edtech funding trough as much as it reflects YC's actual portfolio.

The recent concentration is dramatic. Winter 2025 alone accounts for six companies (Miyagi Labs, Scout, Alice.tech, Excellence Learning, Edexia, and — in the same window — the tail of the Fall 2024 and Winter 2024 cohorts), and Spring 2025 adds three more (Tegore, Chiron, Units), with Summer 2025 contributing Risely AI and Frizzle. Adding Summer 2024 (Educato), Fall 2024 (General Learning), Winter 2024 (Mathos), and Summer 2026 (Bloomy), more than two-thirds of this set was founded in the last two years.

Three themes dominate the recent cohorts. First, AI tutoring for math and science is crowded — Chiron, Excellence Learning, Mathos, and Tegore are all pointed at overlapping problems from different technical angles. Second, teacher-side automation (Edexia, Frizzle) is emerging as the B2B counterweight to student-side tutors. Third, school infrastructure is being re-attempted: Scout and Risely AI are explicitly rebuilding the systems-of-record layer that Clever and Salesforce Education Cloud occupied a decade ago. Founder profiles also skew unusually young in the newest batches — Edexia's founders built a $200K ARR business at 17 and 18, General Learning's CTO is 19, and Units' founder was covered by Forbes at 18 — sitting alongside a second population of very senior operators from McKinsey, Bain, Salesforce, Coinbase, and Airwallex.

RESEARCH NOTES

The most important caveat is that this evidence set is composed of founder profiles, not company records. There are no company websites, no YC directory URLs, and no official company one-liners in the data. Descriptions above are drawn strictly from founders' self-written bios and company names; where a bio is empty (Codecademy, Outschool, GiveCampus, Panorama, Cambly, CleverDeck), the product description should be treated as unverified and confirmed against the YC directory before use.

There is a direct data conflict on CleverDeck: the profile subtitle reads "Winter 2012" while the structured batch field reads "Summer 2009." Justin Cannon's batch should be verified independently. Similarly, Bloomy is tagged Summer 2026, which is either a forward-dated or provisional batch label and warrants confirmation.

One inconsistency worth flagging: Janet Liu's title is recorded as Founder/CMO at General Learning, but her own bio reads "COO @ General Learning." Michael Tong's bio also references RevisionDojo rather than General Learning, suggesting either a rebrand, a parent/product relationship, or stale profile text. Confirm the company's current name and structure before outreach.

Two founders — Sameer Shariff (Cambly) and Justin Cannon (CleverDeck) — have no LinkedIn URL in the evidence, so no contact link is provided for them. Several other URLs are legacy LinkedIn formats (Outschool, GiveCampus) that may no longer resolve.

Coverage gaps to close: this set almost certainly under-represents 2017–2023 education companies, and it contains no company-level metrics, funding data, or headcount. Follow-up angles worth pursuing include (1) mapping which of the four AI math tutors are actually differentiated technically versus by geography and go-to-market, (2) testing whether Scout and Risely AI overlap or address different institutional layers, and (3) determining whether the very young founder cohort (Edexia, General Learning, Units) is a deliberate YC sourcing shift or an artifact of this sample.
